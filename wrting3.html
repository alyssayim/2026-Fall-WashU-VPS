Writing Response 3

When do you cherish the slow or hard way of doing something? Why?

I definitely prefer meticulous and rather painful drawing processes over simpler ones—a big critique that I’ve repeatedly gotten my tendency to go detail-heavy on exercises that are meant to train gesture, composition, or other techniques. I love abstracting things into smaller pieces, shading each part, and making something busy and nonsensical—I almost want to overwhelm the viewer.

But aside from my artistic practice, I also make sure to exercise and manage my food intake. I was recently diagnosed with a couple things, and I found that giving myself specific times of day to do things gives me a lot of structure. I’ve always sort of done this—I take the long way, make sure I’m getting my nutrients in—but I recently built in specific schedules and nutrient goals to reach. It’s definitely a lot harder, but my overall happiness has increased. I ask my friends to exercise with me and try to understand how different macronutrients at different times of day can affect my mood and energy. I think reframing my health into something that isn’t necessarily a fitness goal, but more about energy and mood management definitely made me feel more motivated create structure around my day-to-day life. (I think it might also be because most dopamine rushes really work on me…)

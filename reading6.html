Reading Response 6

This is a reading response for Amisola's There are No Gardens on the Internet.

This website sort of bandaged the "insecurities" I got from the past reading response. I guess I initially felt that my intuitive approach to web design was too illustrative, but this website was very successful in maintaining an illustrative aesthetic while serving its purpose as a website. I like to think about it in comparison to Zucconi's work—to me, they represent two opposite ends of the spectrum. It's difficult to say which one I like more, yet I feel like I always end up in a headspace more akin to Amisola's, while Zucconi moreso takes on an approach I can't seem to rewire my brain into. I loved Amisola's methods of indicating navigation, whether it was playful hints, progress bars, or maps. It all effectively made the user experience much more intuitive without making away from the immersive aspect of the web navigation. I also particularly loved how she achieved movement with elements that were mostly static—the still shots that almost acted like frames to indicate animated movement, as well as smooth bobbing and movements of static elements. I think her approach was fascinating, despite the rather literal approach to her metaphor.
